C · Immersive — the wallpaper is the popover

The live video fills the whole popover; controls float on glass. Transport arrows step the shuffle (prev / next wallpaper). Prettiest of the three and the least information-dense — settings still need a second page behind the gear, and legibility depends on the wallpaper underneath.

Menu bar icon — candidates in situ

1 · Rings — the app icon itself

Broken concentric arcs around a core dot, straight from the Icon Composer artwork. Unmistakably Phosphene, reads at 17 px, and the top-right gap already leaves room for the update dot (right) without crowding.

2 · Play ring

Play triangle in a circle — communicates "video" instantly but is generic; a dozen utilities use some version of it. The safe choice, not the memorable one.

3 · Display + spark

A display outline with a phosphene burst at its center — "light on your screen." Honest about being a wallpaper app, but the inner detail gets muddy at menu bar size.

4 · Burst

The phosphene flash reduced to four rays and a core. Cleanest silhouette of the set, but loses the ring motif that ties it to the app icon.

Recommendation

Popover: B's information architecture in A's clothes. A's hero-with-scrim and status card look the most like a sibling of adrafinil, while B is the one that actually fixes the complaint — nothing hidden, updates and agent-restart demoted to chrome. The merge: A's hero preview and footer, B's toggle pills in place of A's status card (the status card mostly restates what the segmented control and scrim already say). C is the beautiful one; it could be the "compact" appearance later, but as the only UI it buries settings again.

Icon: Rings (1), with the coral badge dot for updates. It's the only candidate that's actually Phosphene's, the arcs survive 17 px because each is a single stroke, and the badge slots into the ring gap. NSStatusItem renders template images, so the dot needs the icon swapped to a pre-badged variant — same pattern most updaters use.
